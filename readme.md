# [DOA+ Interference Detection] Using Two-Dimensional Signal Direction Estimation Technology to Detect Interference Signals [with MATLAB Code]

Introduction: Interference poses a severe threat to wireless networks, exploiting the openness of communication media to cause denial-of-service attacks. By utilizing receivers specifically designed for only receiving modes, passive functions such as monitoring and analyzing input signals can be achieved. In this scenario, the use of two-dimensional (2D) arrival direction (DOA) estimation techniques allows for the localization of interference devices. This method enables the receiver to precisely locate the source of interference without transmitting any signals, making it particularly useful in scenarios where stealth or signal silence is crucial.
To avoid interference attacks, the literature [2] employs rotation-invariant techniques (ESPRIT) and multi-signal classification (MUSIC) algorithms for DOA estimation of interference signals on uniform linear arrays and L-shaped array antennas. In [3], the author improved the detection of interference signals on an uniform circular array (UCA) using a fast orthogonal search method. Additionally, in [4], the authors utilized hyperbolic tangent and UCA to estimate the direction of coherent interference sources. However, these uniform arrays present significant challenges related to limited array aperture and degrees of freedom (DOF). Therefore, [5] employed the augmented receive covariance matrix estimation of interference signals with virtual difference covariance matrices for multi-directional DOA estimation. It focuses on one-dimensional (1D) DOA estimation. Subsequently, in [6], the author considered 2D sparse arrays and sparse signal processing for jamming direction estimation and suppression. Consequently, accurate DOA estimation allows for more precise positioning of signal sources, thereby enhancing the system's ability to distinguish and detect interference signals.
In this study, we investigated two parallel arrays for 2D DOA estimation. In [7] and [9], the authors used the 2D propagator method (PM) and MUSIC estimation method on ULA and UCA respectively. However, these uniform arrays pose significant challenges relative to finite O(N) DOF with N physical sensors. Therefore, in [10]-[13], researchers proposed sparse nested and interleaved arrays to construct a differential covariance array [14] containing O(N2) virtual elements, despite only employing N physical sensors. Then, these CAB-based models applied root MUSIC (RMUSIC) and ESPRIT algorithms to identify sources beyond the number of physical sensors. Moreover, grid size also poses a significant challenge to DOA estimation. A coarse grid leads to estimation errors, while a fine grid significantly increases complexity as shown in [9] and [13]. Besides the methods discussed above, deep learning techniques [15]-[18] have also been widely applied. However, due to their high computational resource requirements, they were not included in this paper. Based on the analysis above, the proposed unstructured method can reduce computational complexity and improve effective degrees of freedom.
Recent research results indicate that even when the SNR is close to infinity, estimation error converges to positive values rather than zero, known as "saturated" behavior [19]-[23]. In fact, the sample-based covariance matrix directly provides an approximate representation of the true covariance. References [24]-[26] suggest that using algorithms that exploit structured covariance matrices instead of relying on sample covariance matrices can improve angle estimation performance. Therefore, many estimation measures that rely on covariance matrix estimation have attracted significant attention. However, they mainly focus on one-dimensional estimation and uniform arrays.
The motivation for this paper is to propose a two-dimensional direction of arrival (DOA) estimation method based on the structured covariance matrix estimation. Unlike traditional CAB methods, our algorithm yields an augmented covariance matrix with a 2-Level Toeplitz (2LT) structure. We introduce the concept of 2LT matrices in the null space of the Hermitian Toeplitz matrix. Notably, Hermitian Toeplitz matrices can be considered as functions of the unknown parameter vector. Consequently, we derive a closed-form solution to estimate the sample covariance matrix of the 2LT structure. The estimated noisy augmented covariance matrix is then subjected to an eigenvalue decomposition (EVD). This method effectively extracts the DOA of interference signals, overcoming the "saturation" issue of non-structured sample covariance matrices in CAB DOA estimation.

Conclusions differ from classical methods of extended covariance matrix-based two-dimensional DOA estimation, which employ a structured covariance matrix with two mutually orthogonal parallel arrays. By estimating the asymptotic maximum likelihood (ML) of the structured covariance matrix, we extend this estimation method to two-dimensional DOA, aiming to obtain a virtual array with an uncorrupted version of the extended covariance matrix. Through improvements to the 2LT covariance matrix structure, the proposed DOA estimator in this paper outperforms traditional two-dimensional DOA estimators under sparse array conditions.
